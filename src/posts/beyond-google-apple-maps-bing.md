Ask most business owners about local SEO in Port Huron and they think about one thing: Google. That makes sense. Google runs the biggest map and the biggest search engine, so it gets almost all the attention. But it is not the only place people search for a business, and the other maps are where you have almost no competition. Apple Maps and Bing both send real customers your way, and while your competitors are fighting over Google, those listings are sitting there wide open.

Claiming them takes an afternoon, costs nothing, and puts your business in front of people you are currently invisible to. Here is how it works and why it is worth your time.

## Why Google Isn't the Whole Local Search Picture

Every iPhone in St. Clair County ships with Apple Maps as the default. When someone taps an address in a text, asks Siri for directions, or searches for a restaurant from their phone, Apple Maps is often what opens, not Google. That is a huge slice of local searches happening on a map you may have never checked.

Bing is smaller, but it quietly powers more than you would guess. It feeds map results into Windows, into Microsoft Copilot, and into a chunk of desktop searches from people who never changed their default browser. Older customers and business users lean on it more than the numbers suggest. Ignoring these two maps means passing on customers who are ready to call or drive over, just because they happened to search somewhere other than Google.

## Claiming Your Apple Maps Listing

Apple handles business listings through a free tool called Apple Business Connect. You create an account, search for your business, and verify that you own it, usually by phone or by matching your details against public records. Once you are in, you can set your hours, add photos, write a short description, and pick the categories that describe what you do.

The verification step is the part people give up on, so plan for it to take a few days. Apple sometimes needs to confirm you are the real owner before it hands over control. Push through it. Once your listing is verified and filled out, your business shows up properly when an iPhone user searches nearby, with the right hours, the right phone number, and a tap-to-call button that actually works.

## Don't Skip Bing Places for Business

Bing has its own free tool, Bing Places for Business, and it has a shortcut most people miss. If your Google Business Profile is already set up, Bing lets you import it directly instead of typing everything in again. That turns a long chore into a five-minute job. You confirm the imported details, add anything Bing is missing, and verify ownership.

Because so few small businesses bother with Bing, a complete and accurate listing there can stand out in a way that is much harder to pull off on crowded Google results. It is low effort for a listing that keeps working in the background.

## Keep Your Details Identical Across Every Map

Here is the rule that ties all of this together. Your business name, address, and phone number have to match exactly everywhere they appear. Not close, exact. If Google says "Suite 200," Apple says "Ste 200," and Bing says nothing at all, search engines lose confidence that these listings describe the same real business, and that costs you in the rankings.

Pick one format and use it everywhere, down to the abbreviations. This is the same consistency work that drives your Google results, and we covered it in our [Michigan local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-michigan/). The good news is that every map you add reinforces the others when the details line up, so the effort compounds instead of splitting your attention.

## A Simple Order to Tackle This

Start with Google, since it is still the biggest and everything else can copy from it. Get your [Google Business Profile ranking in the local map pack](/blog/ranking-in-google-map-pack/) first. Then import that profile into Bing Places in a few minutes. Then set aside an afternoon for Apple Business Connect and its verification wait. In under a week you can go from one map to three, without touching your budget.

None of this is complicated. It is just the kind of quiet, unglamorous work that most local businesses never get around to, which is exactly why doing it puts you ahead.
