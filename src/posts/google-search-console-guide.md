Most Port Huron business owners spend money and effort on local SEO without ever seeing the one report that shows whether it's working. Google Search Console is that report. It's free, Google runs it, and it tells you exactly which searches are putting your business in front of people in St. Clair County.

Analytics tells you what people do once they land on your site. Search Console tells you how they found you in the first place. You need both, but if you only have time for one, this is where the search story starts.

## What Search Console Actually Shows You

Once your site is connected, Search Console answers questions you've probably been guessing at:

- What terms are people typing when your site shows up? You might be ranking for "web designer near me" without knowing it.
- How many times did you appear in search, and how many of those turned into clicks?
- Which of your pages does Google actually send traffic to?
- Is Google having trouble reading any of your pages?

That last one matters more than owners realize. A page can look perfect to you and be invisible to Google because of a technical hiccup. Search Console flags it so you can fix it instead of wondering why the phone isn't ringing.

## Setting It Up Takes About Ten Minutes

Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with a Google account. Add your website as a property. Google needs to confirm you own the site, usually by adding a small verification file or a DNS record. If your site was built properly, this is quick. If you're not sure how to verify, whoever built or hosts your site can do it in a few minutes.

Once you're verified, Google needs a few days to start filling in data. Don't panic if it's empty on day one. Come back in a week and you'll have something to look at.

## The Reports Worth Your Time

You don't need to understand every screen. Focus on these.

### Performance

This is the heart of it. You'll see four numbers: total clicks, total impressions, average click-through rate, and average position. Impressions mean you showed up in search. Clicks mean someone chose you. If you're getting impressions but few clicks, your title and description aren't pulling people in, and that's usually a quick fix.

Scroll down and you'll see the actual search queries. This is gold for a local business. If you're a Port Huron company showing up for the right local terms, your [local SEO](/blog/seo-basics-local-businesses/) is working. If you're showing up for terms that have nothing to do with what you sell, that's a signal your pages are pointed at the wrong keywords.

### Pages

Filter the Performance report by page and you'll see which pages earn their keep. Your service pages and a few strong blog posts should be pulling traffic. If a page you care about is getting nothing, it needs better content, better internal links, or a better title.

### Indexing

This report tells you which pages Google has actually added to search and which it skipped. If an important page says "not indexed," that page cannot rank, period. Catching this early is the difference between a page that quietly works and one that never had a chance.

## What to Do With What You Find

Search Console is only useful if it changes something. Once a month, spend fifteen minutes and ask three questions. Are my impressions and clicks trending up? Am I showing up for local searches that match what I actually do? Is every page I care about indexed and error-free?

Pair that with a quick look at your traffic in [Google Analytics](/blog/understanding-google-analytics/) and you have a clear picture of how people find you and what they do next. That's the whole loop, and it costs nothing but attention.
