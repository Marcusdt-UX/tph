You have seen the photo. A team of impossibly attractive people in a glass conference room, high-fiving over a laptop. Nobody in Port Huron has ever worked in that room. But that photo is sitting on hundreds of small business websites right now, including a few around here.

Stock photography is easy, and that is exactly the problem. When your website looks interchangeable with every other website, visitors have no reason to believe you are any different. Good web design in Port Huron leans on the one thing your competitors cannot copy: your actual business, in your actual town.

## What Stock Photos Quietly Signal

Visitors do not consciously think "that is a stock photo." They just get a vague feeling that something is off, and they leave. A few things are happening under the surface.

Stock photos suggest you have nothing real to show. If a contractor's site has zero photos of finished jobs, people assume there are no finished jobs worth showing. Stock photos also break the connection to place. Somebody searching for a local business wants a local business, and a stock skyline that clearly is not the Blue Water Bridge does not tell them they found one.

Worst of all, the same images circulate widely. Two competing shops in the same county running the same smiling handshake photo is not a hypothetical. We have seen it.

## Real Photos Do Real Work

A photo of your crew standing in front of your truck does more selling than three paragraphs of copy. It answers questions people are already asking: Are these real people? Do they look like they know what they are doing? Would I be comfortable letting them into my house?

Photos of completed work carry the same weight. Before and after shots, finished installs, a busy shop floor, your storefront on a normal Tuesday. This is the visual half of the trust argument we made in [building trust online](/blog/building-trust-online/), and it pairs naturally with written [testimonials and social proof](/blog/social-proof-testimonials-website/). Words say you do good work. Photos show it.

Local landmarks help too, used sparingly. A shot with recognizable local scenery tells a St. Clair County visitor, instantly, that you are nearby. Just do not overdo the postcard shots. One or two is atmosphere. Ten is a tourism site.

## You Do Not Need a Photographer To Start

The bar is lower than most business owners think. A recent phone camera in decent light beats a mediocre DSLR photo every time, and it beats stock photography by a mile.

A few things that make a real difference:

Shoot outdoors or near a window. Natural light solves most problems before you take the shot. Avoid overhead fluorescents, which turn everything a sickly green.

Clean up the frame first. Move the clutter, the empty cups, the random cardboard box. Ten seconds of tidying saves you an hour of editing.

Take far more shots than you need. Fifty photos to get five good ones is a normal ratio, not a failure.

Get people in the frame. Faces outperform empty rooms and product-only shots almost every time.

Shoot horizontal for banners and vertical for social. Doing both while you are set up saves you a second trip.

Once you have a batch you like, a professional shoot every year or two is worth budgeting for. But do not let "we are waiting on a photographer" keep placeholder images on your site for eight months. It happens constantly.

## Optimize Them Or They Will Slow You Down

Real photos come with one real cost: file size. A straight-off-the-phone image can be six megabytes, and a page full of those will crawl on a phone connection. That undercuts everything, since [site speed affects both rankings and conversions](/blog/website-speed-matters/).

Resize images to the dimensions they actually display at, export them as WebP where possible, and enable lazy loading so images below the fold do not load until someone scrolls. Then write a real alt description for each one. Alt text helps screen reader users and gives search engines useful context, and a photo captioned "deck rebuild in Fort Gratiot" is worth far more than one named IMG_4471.

## Where To Put Them

Priority order, if you are starting from nothing: your homepage hero, your about page, and your service or product pages. Those three carry the most traffic and the most doubt. Swap the generic images there first, then work through the rest of the site as you collect more.
