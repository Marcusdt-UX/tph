2. What anke is, and what it isn't

anke gives you a way to organise a lending circle: create or join a group, put money into the group's fund, vote on loans to members, and keep track of what is owed. That is all we provide.

  • Not a bank. Money in your wallet or in a group fund is not a deposit and is not insured by the FDIC or anyone else.
  • Not a lender. We don't make loans, set a group's terms, collect debts or report to credit bureaus.
  • Not a custodian. We never hold your money and have no key to your wallet or to a group fund.
  • Not an adviser. Nothing in anke is financial, legal or tax advice.

3. Your wallet

Your money in anke is held as USDC, a digital dollar issued by Circle, on the Base network, in a wallet that only you control. The wallet is created on your device by our wallet provider. Its key stays on your device.

  • You are responsible for your device and for the recovery methods you set up (such as your email, phone number or a passkey).
  • If you lose your device and every recovery method, we cannot recover your wallet for you. See section 5 for what your group can do.
  • Transactions on the Base network can't be reversed, by us or by anyone.

4. Adding money and cashing out

Buying USDC with a card or bank account, and turning USDC back into dollars in your bank account, is done by our payment partner, not by us. They will ask you to confirm your identity and you will agree to their terms. They set their own fees and limits and can decline a transaction. We never receive or hold your dollars.

5. Group funds

A group's pooled money sits in a smart contract on the Base network: a program that follows fixed rules and that the group's members control by voting. Our own roles on it are limited to pausing new activity for up to 14 days for safety, and clearing a seat that is empty and unused. Neither lets us move anyone's money.

  • Voting. Members vote in proportion to what they have in the fund beyond what they owe it. The group votes on loans, on admitting a new member's wallet, on sharing the fund's earnings and on closing the fund.
  • Leaving. You can take your share out by giving notice. The group can approve it at once. If they don't, you can take it out after 30 days. You must repay any loan first. What you receive is what your share is worth when you take it, which can be less than you put in if the group has had losses.
  • A lost wallet. If a member's wallet has not been used for 60 days, two-thirds of the other members can vote to move that member's place in the fund to a new wallet, after a 7-day wait. Using the old wallet in any way stops this. Only vote for it when you are sure, in person, who you are helping.
  • Limits. A fund holds up to 50 members. Each member can have up to two loans or requests open.

6. Loans between members

  • A loan is an agreement between the borrower and the other members of the group. We are not a party to it.
  • The contract charges simple interest by the day at the yearly rate your group chose when the fund was created, never more than 7% a year. Loans run for at least 61 days. Repaying early costs less.
  • Interest is paid into the group's fund. We take none of it.
  • If a loan is more than 14 days overdue, anyone can mark it in default. The borrower's own share of the fund is used to cover it first. Anything still unpaid is a loss shared by the group's members.
  • A member with an unpaid defaulted loan can't borrow again, and the fund doesn't take new money until the default is repaid or, after 90 days, written off.
  • Only lend what you can afford to lose, to people you know.
